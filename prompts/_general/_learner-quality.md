## Shared learner-quality policy

These are authoring and review instructions, not extra output sections. Keep the
phase's output format, machine fields, answer markers and notation contract intact.

- **This lesson only.** Teach and test only the supplied lesson. Family templates
  apply only when that method is taught and its prerequisites and data are supplied.
  Otherwise use this lesson's actual concept, classification, relation or procedure.
  Define any necessary new term briefly before requiring its use. Do not import a
  neighbouring lesson's methods or invent facts to make a template work.
- **Visible evidence.** Supply all necessary evidence and premises in the content
  the student can see before answering. Hidden keys, rubrics, metadata, feedback
  revealed only after answering, and previous student answers are not evidence.
  A source-category name (coin, chronicle, experiment) alone is not proof.
  If asking about provenance or purpose, show an identified excerpt with only its
  supplied author/maker, date and source type; ask only what that evidence supports.
  Never invent authors, quotations, data or provenance. If no suitable source is
  supplied, use clearly labelled lesson information cards containing the actual
  lesson facts; do not dress a lesson summary up as a historical source.
- **Checks and results are different.** Choosing a useful source or test is not
  observing its result. Supply results before requiring a conclusion. In a fixed
  sequence, give the same necessary results to everyone, whatever they selected.
  Keep each later choice and follow-up answerable after any earlier choice; restate
  needed facts rather than asking the learner to rely on a previous answer.
- **Fair assessment.** Align each graded component with visible evidence and the
  actual question. Every single-answer choice or blank must have exactly one
  defensible answer: no synonymous alternatives, equivalent forms, subset answers,
  overlapping categories, or another interpretation that makes a distractor true.
  Do not signal correctness by length, grammar fit, register or style alone.
  For free-text corrections/explanations accept substantively equivalent answers;
  uniqueness of choices is not a demand for one exact wording in free text.
- **Meaning fidelity.** Preserve approximate quantities, modal strength (may,
  usually), geographic scope, chronology and other qualifiers. Do not silently
  turn an estimate into an exact fact, sequence into causation, or a possibility
  into certainty. Preserve source terms, quantities, units and typed mathematics
  and chemistry notation. Apply a lesson correction only when explicitly supplied
  for that lesson; do not generalize it to other lessons.
- **Age and language.** Follow the language contract, including L2 target language
  versus scaffolding language. For grades 1–6 use short, concrete instructions and
  familiar roles, while keeping respectful Uzbek Siz (Russian Вы when applicable).
  Avoid professional or bureaucratic wording and never expose authoring jargon
  such as rubric, evidence chain, distractor or organizational-level diagnosis to
  the learner unless that term is itself taught. Keep instructions respectful at
  every grade. Use the lower existing recall/cloze counts for grades 1–6, and fewer
  distinct items when the lesson cannot support them; never pad with repeats.
- **Real application.** The application challenge must use a different situation
  and a different use of the lesson knowledge from the preview, not the same case
  with names changed. Both must remain within the taught lesson.
- **Present references only.** Refer only to text, data and visuals actually present
  in the student material. A requested or pending image is not visible evidence:
  also supply the facts in text when an answer depends on it. Optional reflection
  uses actual supplied facts, never an absent map, chart or source.
