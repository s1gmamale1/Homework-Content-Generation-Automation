  The setup (think of a kitchen)

  - There's one shared order ticket rail — a single list of work that needs doing. That's the database
  (the "head").
  - There are worker computers — these are the cooks. In your real setup, ~10 PCs. Each cook walks up
  to the rail, grabs the next ticket, cooks it, comes back for another. They never collide because
  each ticket can only be grabbed by one cook.

  What "launching a subject" does

  When you launch a subject (say History), the system reads that textbook's lessons and pins one
  ticket per lesson onto the rail — "make the homework packet for Lesson 1," "...Lesson 2," and so on.
  26 lessons → 26 tickets.

  That's all launching does: it puts work on the rail. It does not create cooks. This is the part that
  was confusing you — launching a subject fills the funnel (the colored bars, the lesson list), but
  it has nothing to do with the worker cards.

  What a worker (cook) actually does in the background

  When a cook computer is turned on, three things happen automatically, forever, on their own:

  1. It signs in. The moment it starts, it writes its name on a whiteboard: "DESKTOP-07 is here." →
  That's what creates its card on your dashboard.
  2. It raises its hand every 30 seconds. Like saying "still here, still here, still here." The
  whiteboard keeps a timestamp of the last time it spoke.
  3. It grabs tickets and cooks. It pulls a lesson off the rail, runs the AI to generate that
  homework, marks the ticket done, grabs the next one.

  How the dashboard decides green vs grey

  Your dashboard just looks at the whiteboard and does simple math:
  - "DESKTOP-07 spoke 12 seconds ago" → green dot, online.
  - If a cook crashes or you turn it off, it stops raising its hand. After 90 seconds of silence, the
  dashboard greys out its card automatically. Nobody has to clean up — silence = offline.

  So why were your worker cards empty?

  Because no cook was running. I started only the "front desk" (the website + the order rail), with
  the cooks switched off, so nothing was signing in on the whiteboard. The funnel had data (I'd pinned
  tickets on the rail), but the whiteboard was blank.

  The one-sentence version

  ▎ Launching a subject = putting tickets on the rail (fills the funnel). Worker cards = cooks signing
  ▎ into the whiteboard, which only happens when a cook computer is actually running. They're two
  ▎ separate things.

  ---
  So to make a worker card appear, you don't launch anything — you turn on a cook. On your real fleet,
  that's a PC running the worker program; on this test box, it's one program I (or you) start.

  Here's the choice that matters: when a real cook turns on, it will start cooking the tickets already
  on the rail — i.e. it actually runs the AI and spends real quota.
